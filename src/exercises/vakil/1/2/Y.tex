IMPORTANT EXERCISE THAT YOU SHOULD DO ONCE IN YOUR LIFE (YONEDA'S LEMMA). (a) Suppose you have two objects $A$ and $A'$ in a category $\mathcal{C}$, and maps (1.2.11.2) $i_C : \operatorname{Mor}(C,A) \to \operatorname{Mor}(C,A')$ that commute with the maps (1.2.11.1). Show that the $i_C$ (as $C$ ranges over the objects of $\mathcal{C}$) are induced from a unique morphism $g : A \to A'$. More precisely, show that there is a unique morphism $g : A \to A'$ such that for all $C \in \mathcal{C}$, $i_C$ is $u \mapsto g \circ u$. (b) If furthermore the $i_C$ are all bijections, show that the resulting $g$ is an isomorphism. (Hint for both: This is much easier than it looks. This statement is so general that there are really only a couple of things that you could possibly try. For example, if you're hoping to find a morphism $A \to A'$, where will you find it? Well, you are looking for an element $\operatorname{Mor}(A,A')$. So just plug $C=A$ into (1.2.11.2), and see where the identity goes.)
