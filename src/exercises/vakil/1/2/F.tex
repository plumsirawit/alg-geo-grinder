EXERCISE. (a) Show that localization commutes with finite products, or equivalently, with finite direct sums. In other words, if $M_1,\ldots,M_n$ are $A$-modules, describe an isomorphism (of $A$-modules and of $S^{-1}A$-modules)
$$
S^{-1}(M_1\times\cdots\times M_n)\longrightarrow S^{-1}M_1\times\cdots\times S^{-1}M_n.
$$
(b) Show that localization commutes with arbitrary direct sums. (c) Show that localization does not necessarily commute with infinite products: the obvious map
$$
S^{-1}\left(\prod_i M_i\right)\longrightarrow\prod_i S^{-1}M_i
$$
induced by the universal property of localization is not always an isomorphism. (Hint: $(1,1/2,1/3,1/4,\ldots)\in\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}\times\cdots$.)
